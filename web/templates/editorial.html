Editorial standards

These are the rules every guide on RenterLaw follows. They are how you can tell what backs a statement, and how much to trust it.

Every statement links to its source

A guide is a list of statements. Each statement carries one or more source chips. A chip links to the exact document the statement comes from. Our drafting software will not save a citation unless the quoted words appear in that document word for word.

The chip tells you what kind of source it is:

  • § Statute or regulation. The law itself. This is the strongest source.
  • 🏛 Government. A court, agency, or city page explaining a rule or a program.
  • ⚖ Court ruling. A decision by a court.
  • Org. A legal aid group or nonprofit describing its own services. We use these for local help listings, not for what the law says.
  • Ed. Editorial guidance. See below.

Editorial guidance

A small number of statements are practical advice, not law. For example: put every request to your landlord in writing and keep a copy. Take dated photos before you move in.

Advice like this is what legal aid lawyers tell renters everywhere. It cannot be traced to one section of law, so it carries the gray Editorial guidance chip instead of a source. We only publish it when it applies in every place and matches what legal aid groups recommend. If a statement can be backed by a statute instead, we do that and drop the label.

How a guide is checked

Software researches the law and writes the first draft. It will not save a citation unless the quoted words appear in the source word for word.

A second reader, also software, reads each statement against its source. It records the exact passage that backs the statement. If it cannot find one, the statement is fixed and read again, or held for a person to decide.

Every change to a statement is logged, with who made it and the rule that allowed it.

A person publishes every page. Nothing goes live without that step. The line at the top of each guide says who published it and when.

Sources are re-checked over time

Laws change. We fetch each source again every week and compare it to what we quoted. When every source behind a statement still matches, the statement shows a "Sources checked" date. When the words we quoted are gone, the statement loses that date and the guide is flagged for review.

Plain language

Guides are written for a renter who is stressed, short on time, and reading on a phone. Short sentences. Common words. When a legal term appears, we explain it in the same sentence. Deadlines say what starts the clock. Percentages come with a worked dollar example.

Not legal advice

No statement on this site is legal advice for your situation, whether it is backed by a statute or marked as editorial guidance. If your housing is at risk, contact a lawyer or a legal aid office near you.

Tell us when something is wrong

Every guide has a link to report a problem. A person reads every report and checks the source before changing anything.
