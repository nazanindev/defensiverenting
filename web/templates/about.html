About RenterLaw

Tenant law is public, but it is spread across statutes, regulations, and government PDFs. It is written for lawyers. It is hardest to read at the moment you need it.

RenterLaw is a free guide to your rights as a renter. Guides are organized by place and by situation. Every statement links to the law it comes from, so you can read the law yourself instead of taking our word for it.

What is on this site

Guides. One guide per place and situation: repairs, deposits, rent increases, eviction notices, and more. Each guide is written for a specific city, state, or the whole country. See every place we cover.

Legal terms. The words on a lease or a court notice, explained in plain language, with the rule in every place we cover. Browse the terms.

Local help. Some problems need a person, not a page. Where we can, a guide links to the legal aid groups, rent assistance programs, and city offices in that city.

How a guide gets made

Tenant law covers 50 states and thousands of cities. 2 people cannot read and write all of it by hand. So software does the research and the first draft. It finds the law, reads it, and quotes it. It cannot save a quote unless those exact words appear in the source. A made up citation cannot enter the site.

Then a person checks it. Every statement is read against the source it cites before the guide goes live. The byline on each guide says who checked it and when.

We re-check sources over time. If a law changes and the words we quoted are gone, the guide is flagged for review.

What this site is not

This is not legal advice. We can explain what the law says. We cannot tell you what to do in your situation. If your housing is at risk, contact a lawyer or a legal aid office near you.
