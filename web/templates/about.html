About Defensive Renting

Tenant law is public — but for most renters, it might as well be hidden. It's fragmented across statutes, regulations, and government PDFs, written for lawyers, and nearly impossible to navigate when you're already in a stressful situation.

Defensive Renting is a free, citation-backed guide to tenant rights, organized by city and situation. Every statement on this site links directly to its primary source — a statute, regulation, or government document — so you can read what the law actually says, not just what someone summarized.

Who we are

We're a two-person team — a software engineer and a writer, both CMU alumni. One of us builds and maintains the platform; the other researches the law and writes the guides.

We're not a law firm, a nonprofit, or a legal aid organization. We're two people who believe access to legal information should be easier than it currently is.

How the content is made

Every guide is researched from primary sources — state statutes, city ordinances, and government publications. Our writer reads the actual law, writes plain-language summaries, and links each claim to the section it came from. The platform enforces this: there is no way to publish a statement without a citation attached.

Content marked Editorial guidance is an exception — practical advice that's widely accepted but can't be traced to a single statute. Read more about how we use that label.

What this site is not

This is not legal advice. If you are facing eviction, a habitability dispute, or any situation where your housing is at risk, contact a lawyer or your local legal aid organization. We can help you understand what the law says. We cannot tell you what to do in your specific situation.
