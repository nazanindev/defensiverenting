Content Guidelines

Read once before your first page.

Topic and page type

Every page has both, and you choose them separately.

  • Topic — what the page is about. A fixed list: pick one, you can't invent one.
  • Page type — how it's laid out. Playbook or Directory.

They're independent. A Directory page on the Can't Pay Rent topic is correct if the honest answer is a list of places to go. Pittsburgh has one.

Enforced The Local Help topic must use the Directory type. Saving it as a Playbook is rejected: that layout renders organizations as a numbered legal argument.
Playbook
Explains what the law says. Each statement is one citable fact.
Heat Not Working in Boston · Security Deposits in Chicago
Directory
Lists organizations that help. Each statement is one org: what it does, how to reach it, its own URL.
Local Help in Pittsburgh
Local help goes on one page per city Never add a "where to get help" section to a playbook. Seven topics carrying the same referral list means one dead phone number is seven fixes.

Statements

  • One fact per statement. Past 3 sentences, split it
  • Plain English, for someone in a stressful situation
  • Concrete: "must respond within 14 days", not "should respond promptly"
  • No summaries or introductions — those go in the intro field
  • No facts that apply everywhere — check the parent jurisdiction first

Citations

Every statement cites at least one source.

  • Statute — the section of law (Mass. Gen. Laws c. 239, § 8A)
  • Regulation — an administrative rule (105 CMR 410.200)
  • Gov guidance — a government guide, court self-help page, or official FAQ
  • Nonprofit — a legal aid organization's guide. Prefer a statute where one exists
  • Editorial — advice with no traceable law. Rare, and only when genuinely universal

The quote

Paste the exact line from the source, unedited. It is what lets the source checker tell you a law changed: it re-fetches each source and reports when your quoted words are gone.

  • Don't paraphrase it to fit your statement — the statement is your words, the quote is theirs
  • Don't leave it blank. A page with a quote-less citation cannot be published

The locator

A statute must name its provision: § 15B, RCW 59.18.060. A save without one is rejected. If there's no provision to point at, it isn't a statute — change the kind.

This catches a locator naming no provision. It cannot catch one naming the wrong provision. That part is still on you.

Never cite an aggregator needhelppayingbills.com, general HUD landing pages, Wikipedia, or anything else summarizing other sources. Go to the original. In a directory, every organization cites its own URL — if you can't find it, leave the org out.

Titles and intros

  • Title: how a renter would search for it. The form suggests one; edit freely
  • Intro: 1–2 sentences that orient, not a summary. "No heat in winter is an emergency. Here are your rights."
  • Don't write "Not legal advice" — it's added automatically

Before publishing a directory

  • Confirm each org still operates and serves this jurisdiction
  • Check phone numbers and addresses are current
  • No national orgs without a confirmed local presence
  • No duplicates — the same org listed under a different name

Publishing

  • New pages save as drafts and stay off the public site until you publish
  • Editing a published page goes live on save — there is no second confirmation
  • Unsure about a source? Leave it out. A shorter accurate page beats a longer shaky one
