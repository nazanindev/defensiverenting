Content Guidelines

How to write for Defensive Renting. Read this before authoring your first page.

Page types

Every page you create is one of two types. Choose based on what the page is doing — explaining the law, or pointing to help.

Playbook
Step-by-step legal rights guide. Each statement is one citable fact backed by a statute, regulation, or government document. This is the core format of the site — use it for anything that explains what the law says or what a renter's rights are.
Examples: Notice to Quit in Pittsburgh, Heat Not Working in Boston, Security Deposit Not Returned in Chicago
Directory
A listing of organizations or services that can help renters with a specific problem. Each statement is one org — what they do, how to reach them, and a link to their official website. Do not use an aggregator site as the source; find the org's own URL.
Examples: Rental Assistance Resources in Pittsburgh, Legal Aid Organizations in Boston

Writing a playbook

Statements

Each statement is one citable fact. Keep it focused — if you need more than 3 sentences, split it into two statements.

  • Write in plain English for someone in a stressful situation, not a lawyer
  • One fact per statement — what the law says, what the renter is entitled to, what the landlord must do
  • Use concrete language: "Your landlord must respond within 14 days" not "Your landlord should respond promptly"
  • Don't write summaries or topic introductions as statements — those go in the intro field
  • Don't repeat general facts that apply everywhere — check if the statement already exists for a parent jurisdiction

Citations

Every statement must cite at least one primary source. Here's what counts:

  • Statute — the actual section of law (e.g. Mass. Gen. Laws c. 239, § 8A)
  • Regulation — an administrative rule (e.g. 105 CMR 410.200)
  • Gov guidance — a government-published tenant guide, court self-help page, or official FAQ
  • Nonprofit — a legal aid organization's published guide. Use sparingly — prefer statutes when you can find them
  • Editorial — practical advice that can't be traced to a specific law. Use rarely and only for genuinely universal advice
What not to cite Aggregator sites (e.g. needhelppayingbills.com), general HUD landing pages, Wikipedia, or any site that is itself summarizing other sources. Go to the original.

Title and intro

  • Title: written as a question a renter would search — the form auto-suggests one, edit freely
  • Intro: 1–2 sentences that orient the reader. "No heat in winter is an emergency. Here are your rights." Not a summary of what follows
  • Don't include "Not legal advice" in your text — it's added automatically

Writing a directory

Each entry (statement) should include

  • Organization name — bold it in the statement body
  • What they do in 1–2 sentences
  • How to reach them: phone number, address, or website
  • Citation: the organization's own official website — not a third-party directory
The most common mistake Citing one aggregator site (like needhelppayingbills.com) as the source for every entry. Each organization must cite its own URL. If you can't find an org's official site, leave it out.

Before you publish a directory

  • Verify each org still operates and serves the jurisdiction
  • Check that phone numbers and addresses are current
  • Don't list national organizations unless you've confirmed they have a local presence
  • Don't duplicate entries — check for the same org listed under a different name

General

Draft vs published

Every new page saves as a draft and won't appear on the public site until you click Publish. Use drafts to get feedback before going live. Once published, the city will appear on the homepage.

Editing published pages

You can edit a published page at any time. Changes go live immediately on save — there's no re-publish step. Be careful with published pages; treat edits like they're already live.

When in doubt

If you're unsure whether a source is good enough, default to leaving it out rather than publishing something you can't verify. A shorter, accurate page is better than a longer one with a shaky citation.
