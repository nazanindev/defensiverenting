# Site copy

Rules for every word on the site that is not a statement: page titles, headings, intros, buttons, links, form labels and help text, error and empty messages, banners, emails, and meta descriptions. Statements follow SKILL.md. Everything in SKILL.md applies here too (no em dashes, short sentences, plain words, digits, "you"). This file adds what is different about site copy.

The reader is the same: stressed, on a phone, maybe not a native English speaker. Site copy is what they read before they find their answer, so every extra word is in the way.

## Who is talking

1. **The site is "we". The reader is "you".** "We check every source each week." Never "RenterLaw checks", never "users", "visitors", or "tenants" for the reader.
2. **"Renter", not "tenant", in anything a reader reads as a sentence.** "Tenant" stays only where people search for it: meta descriptions and the browser title ("tenant rights guide"). Legal names keep their words ("tenant union").
3. **We are 2 people, and we say so.** No "our team", no "our experts", no corporate plural. Name the person when it matters ("A person publishes every guide").

## What we promise

4. **Say only what is true today.** Each trust claim is one of three facts, worded the same everywhere: every answer links to where it comes from; we check those sources every week; a person publishes every guide. Never "verified", "accurate", "up to date", "trusted" or "expert-reviewed".
5. **Say what we cannot do, plainly, where it matters.** "We cannot give legal advice." "We cannot promise a date." Put it next to the thing it limits (a contact form, a reply), not in a footer only.
6. **Privacy lines list exactly what we store.** "We store your email address and your chosen location, and nothing else." Then what we do not do: "We do not sell or share anything you write." No "we take your privacy seriously".
7. **No selling.** No exclamation marks, no "free!", no "amazing", no urgency. "RenterLaw is free and has no ads." is the whole pitch.

## Pieces of a page

8. **Guide titles are the renter's search question**, in title case, place included: "Heat or AC Not Working in Ohio: What Can I Do?" (decided 2026-09-08). Rules pages: "Security Deposit Rules in Ohio: What Does the Law Say?" (ADR-028).
9. **Every other heading is sentence case and short**: "Who we are", "Report a problem", "Legal terms, explained". A heading names what is under it. It is not a slogan.
10. **Headings over statements use the renter's words** (ADR-028 D10). A stage heading is where the renter is in the process: "Ask for your deposit back". A question heading is the question as a renter would type it: "Does my landlord have to give me a receipt?". Write "Does my landlord have to", not "Must my landlord". Write "Can I", not "Am I permitted". Replace legal grammar ("must", "illegally", "in the event of", "respond"), never an official term.
11. **An official term in a heading keeps its name and gets a gloss**, the same rule as statements (SKILL.md rule 8). A renter who does not know "foreclosure" also does not know the bank is the one who takes the building, and they will meet the word on a letter or a court paper. So: "What happens to my lease in a foreclosure (when the bank takes the building from the owner)?", not "What happens if the bank takes my building?". The place goes after the gloss on a rules page. Lawyer words a renter never sees on paper (mitigate, habitability) get plain words instead. The gloss itself uses only everyday words and never explains one legal idea with another: "appeal (ask a different court to look at my case again)", not "appeal (ask a higher court to change the decision)".
12. **Buttons say what happens, verb first**: "Email me a sign-in link", "Send report", "Pick another". Never "Submit", "OK", "Click here", "Learn more".
13. **Links say where they go**: "Read the full guide for Ohio.", "See every place we cover". The link text alone must make sense, because screen readers list links by their text. An arrow (→) only on a link that leads to a list or another section.
14. **Form help says why we ask and what happens.** "We can only reply if you give us an address." "Only if your report is about a group we list." Mark what is optional in words ("Only if you want a reply"), not with an asterisk.
15. **Errors say what went wrong and what to do next**, without blame: "That email address does not look right. Check it and try again." Never "Invalid input" or "Error".
16. **Empty states say why it is empty and what to do**: "We do not have the rule for Ohio yet. The general rule below still applies to you." Never a blank space, never "No results".
17. **Confirmations say what happened and what comes next**: "We got it. A person will check it against the source."

## Words

18. **Same word for the same thing, everywhere.**

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| guide | playbook, article |
| statement (only on editorial and about pages) | claim, fact, entry |
| source | citation, reference, authority |
| place, location, where you rent | jurisdiction |
| legal aid | legal services, pro bono |
| local help | resources, resource directory |
| legal terms | glossary, concepts |
| report a problem | flag, feedback |
| sign in | log in, login |

19. **Internal words never reach a reader**: playbook, statement key, concept, jurisdiction, stamp, queue, draft (except the draft preview banner), topic, slug.
20. **Names are written one way**: RenterLaw (one word, two capitals). DC, not D.C. "All 50 states and DC".

## Meta descriptions and the browser title

21. **The browser title is the page title, then " · RenterLaw".** Meta descriptions are one or two plain sentences under 160 characters: what the page answers and for where, then one trust fact. Search words like "tenant rights" may appear here (rule 2).

## Checking site copy

A quick scan of a template or handler string: search for "—", "!", "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more", "tenant", "jurisdiction", "playbook", "please note", "simply", "easily", "just". Read each heading alone and ask whether a renter would type it or say it out loud.
